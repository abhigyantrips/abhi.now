I have this deep appreciation for artists that are able to take a real-life scene, their perspective of it, and put it down on a flat canvas. Being able to understand the exact slant of 2D lines to communicate the angle from which you were looking at a certain thing leaves me in awe.

This awe extends to software from the early 2000's, where developers were replicating the look and feel of real-life hardware into software with perfect accuracy; from the shadow of every button to the sound of every click.

![A collection of how different music players looked back then, via danq.me.](./images/my-love-for-naive-design.png)

Dan Q has a very cozy post showing the different interfaces from the time I'm talking about, but he surprisingly didn't like how bulky they were or how much space they took.

I recently came across this term called "Naïve Design", referring to the trend of "anti-perfect" design elements that have come up in the recent year as "The Rise Against The Machines".

### Handwritten Fonts

- the loveliest
- my use of sour gummy
- other fonts i absolutely love
- the idea of converting your own handwriting into a font.

### Doodle Icons

- the ones i use, by Khushmeen
- simplicity brings accessibility, but doodle brings character
- not compromising accessibility by making full use of aria
- the idea of "handwritten" art made by other artists to be used instead of generating slop to mimic humans.

### Human Physics

- human visual perception through experience of human things
- torn paper, how it's replicated through SVG textures
- rough notations, how they mimic the unevenness of human handwriting
