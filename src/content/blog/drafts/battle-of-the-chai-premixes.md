Hello! I like _chai_ a lot, as you [might know](/blog/the-perfect-chai). I also like making it; as soon as I learned how to grate _adrak_ and boil milk, I became the staple person to make _chai_ for the family. It could just be that no one else wants to do it, but I like to think it's because I make it so well. :P

Ever since I shifted to hostel though, getting my hands on a stove and a boiler has been... "difficult" might be an understatement. The hostel declared that stuff as basically illegal. But I've had to look for many alternatives to get that same satisfaction that I get after a sip of hot _chai_. So far, the only solution I've reached is _chai premixes_.

![A really aesthetic picture I took for this post.](./images/battle-of-the-chai-premixes-01.jpg)

I've tried many of them on and off throughout hostel. I remember there were times I grimaced at the taste of the _chai_ or even greatly enjoyed it, but I never really kept track of which ones I liked/disliked. Now that I've graduated, I feel like I have an obligation to reach a conclusive staple, for my own future grocery runs. So let's do that.

For both our sakes though, I'm not going to italicize _chai_ for the rest of the post. We both know I'm referring to tea, so let's keep it that way.

### Namaste Chai

![Namaste Chai's "Kadak Chai" premix. They have a large variety, from Lemongrass to Ginger to Elaichi too.](./images/battle-of-the-chai-premixes-02.jpg)

This was one of my favourites in college, and I was worried I'd set some high expectations if I started with this one. But it was already in my inventory, so I proceeded with testing it.

Namaste Chai always has a very strong aroma for every flavour. You might find this too much, but I personally found it calming as I had chai after a long day of classes/work.

### Chaayos

![The latest line-up from Chaayos, as far as I can tell.](./images/battle-of-the-chai-premixes-03.jpg)

![The Chaayos premix I used to have in college. I can't find it in a bigger quantity than a Pack of 4 anymore.](./images/battle-of-the-chai-premixes-06.jpg)

### Red Label

I kinda like Red Label as a brand, in a purely aesthetic sense. I mentioned in a previous blogpost that our family was trying out their _chaipatti_ as an alternative to Taj Mahal, which is still an experiment in progress. But I was excited to have their chai premix.

After making the first cup and drinking it without an accompaniment (biscuits or otherwise), I was left frowning at my mug. The colour and smell were fine, but the taste itself felt like it was _artificially sweetened_.

And not even in a way that would improve the chai experience! The concoction tasted how those yellow SugarFree&trade; powders used to smell. I was quite disappointed by this one.

### Tata Tea Gold

This one, much like Red Label, came across my radar as a brand that makes normal _chaipatti_ but is expanding to other forms of chai. The packaging was very cool and all, now I just had to boil some water and taste it.

![Tata Tea Gold's "Masala Chai" premix. I believe they also have Cardamom and Ginger variants.](./images/battle-of-the-chai-premixes-04.jpg)

The taste is okay, but I _think_ there's something wrong with this one? The chai itself is nice and masala-y, and doesn't have any noticeable amount of sugar. But every time I make this chai, I've noticed there's a ring of red residue left at the top of the mug.

![The red/orange ring in question.](./images/battle-of-the-chai-premixes-05.jpg)

The premix, per calculation, contains about 7 grams of sugar for every 14 gram serve. I couldn't for the life of me taste any reasonable sugar inside the thing though.

I've made sure it's not something that was already in the mug. From what I've observed though, it's the foam from the premix that first starts out orange and then later on shifts to red. I don't know if this points to how real or synthetic the premix is, but I'm probably not going to buy this one again. Ever.
