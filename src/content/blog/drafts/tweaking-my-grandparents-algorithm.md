An app that lets you keep up with life updates from your friends and family. An app that can let you connect with friends from all the different places you've been to in your life, and share your own precious moments with them.

It's no wonder my grandparents loved Facebook, because it lit up their post-retirement life with a lively photo album that held all the memories they find dear. You could leave a comment on someone's post as if sending a letter, and the app would politely remind you of a Friend's birthday so you could wish them. It was a happy place.

### Turbulence

Then came "[Stories](https://www.facebook.com/help/862926927385914/)", "[Feeds](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/fb-feed/)", "[Marketplace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Facebook_features#Marketplace)" and "[Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook_Watch)". An effort to increase Facebook's user-base and engagement. There were more tabs at the top and more X's to click to reach their photo album, but my grandparents still found the app worthy of their attention; if they could just look past all the distractions.

Oh wait, nevermind, the feed now has suggested content. Every fourth post is a public noticeboard in front of your eyes where anyone can put whatever they want. But don't worry, Facebook will try and make sure you like it; because you're more likely to pay attention to it that way.

Many moons ago, these changes were the beginning of the arc of misinformation and "WhatsApp Forwards".

### Toning It Down

Something beautiful to note about the Algorithm is that Meta has very little control over it. Every single setting for tuning your content recommendation is obscure with the hope that you don't use an "Off" button against them.

You don't want to see anything political anymore? Too bad, the best we can do is _stop_ suggesting it. What's the difference between us suggesting something and an unknown post appearing in your feed? Who knows! Both of them are _not_ from the people you follow.

But what about graphic content? The type that has as much effect on a senior citizen as it would on a child? Well, we can't promise anything there either. Even if Meta recognizes a post as sensitive, **they will not guarantee that it doesn't get suggested to you**. How _stupid_ is that? It's not, "if we see something's too much we'll hide it", it's "even if we see something is _exactly_ what you _don't_ want to see, we'll just reduce how much you see it".

### Old

My grandparents love Facebook.

Ah, "love" isn't the right word. "Attachment" probably works better. And no, that's not the same as "addiction".

"Addiction" in the context of social media refers to its ability to act as a constant distraction from constructive tasks. That can be work, studying, errands and whatnot. The average teen/adult has enough of these tasks that hours of screen time can radically stunt their productivity and cognitive ability to grow. They have "work to get to".

But a retired senior citizen living with their children and access to quick commerce _doesn't_ have anywhere to go or anything to critically get done. There's apps for every service and websites for every bank procedure.

Some of these are genuinely helpful for them! I'd rather _Nannoo_ did not have to carry heavy vegetables all the way from the market to his home.
