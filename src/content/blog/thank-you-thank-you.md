---
title: Thank You, Thank You
description: Why *can't* we reply to flowers with flowers?
date: 2026-08-11
---

I recently came across multiple stand-up clips from multiple comedians, talking about how Indians are unique (and "weird", as implied) in replying to a "thank you" with a "thank you" instead of "you're welcome". I'd noticed and understood this before but hadn't actually taken it out of the ordinary.

Why _do_ we reply to a "thank you" with a "thank you", and why did it make sense to me? I'm going to try and answer that with real-life examples that I've experienced myself.

First off, I've mostly only given a thank-you reply during two-way transactions -- where I am providing something and receiving something else in return. So a vendor could thank me for paying them, and I could be thanking them for their service.

A one-way transaction would be a situation where I am helping someone out or doing a favour, in which case the response would be something like "no problem" or "_haanji_". I don't thank someone back because I did not receive (nor did I expect) anything in return.

That's the most theoretical I can get about it though, so I'll just list out the examples.

> A note to my friends: You've probably seen me text "thank you thank you" to you before. That's a different thing, where I type the same thing twice for emphasis ~because I'm cool~. So just keep that in mind!

### Exhibit A: Scanning Boarding Passes

When I stand in line while boarding my flight, and the attendant scans my boarding pass for me, they say "thank you". They thank me for... cooperating, I guess? And I thank them for letting me board onto the plane.

### Exhibit B: Air Hosts/Hostesses on a Plane

(You can tell I travelled by air recently.)

When I'm de-boarding and pass by the air hostesses, I thank them for their service. They thank me back for flying with their company/group. It might be more of an obligatory thanks, but it's still a thank-you reply.

### Exhibit C: Leaving a Cab

The driver ends the trip on whichever app they're using, and show me the UPI QR code. I finish the payment, tell them it's done, and thank them for the ride. They thank me back for making the payment.

### Exhibit D: Shopkeeper Bhaiyas

This is actually a hit-or-miss. I've been to _kirana_ shops where shopkeepers are older uncles that are not very happy to see a GenZ kid. The thank-yous there usually receive a deep grunt in response. But then there are also very nice aunties and _bhaiyas_ that say it back and balance out the uncles.

---

A lot of the other scenarios I can think of revolve around paying for an item or a service. The reception of the money initiates a thank-you, while the person who received the service also says thank-you for it.

So while this post started with a lot of gusto, I invite you to add your own examples or showcase situations where we (as Indians) revert to the normal "you're welcome" response. Thanking someone is almost always a polite and good thing to do, so a couple more flowers given to people here and there should only bring about good!
