---
title: A Cantonment Road
description: A place that only seems to exist in my head, though I can't place why.
date: 2026-09-18
---

There's a place I go to in my head sometimes. An empty, curving road. It has those blinking red lights running along its center, the way roads in Army Cantonments have. I can also feel I'm in an Army Cantt., though I can't tell which one. It could be Shankar Vihar or Jalandhar or anywhere else. The streetlamps are those old ones with bulbous heads, except the yellow halogen lamps have been switched out for cold, white ones.

The place is stuck in nighttime. You can hear all the different bugs you usually do on a cold night like this, with only grass (or maybe trees) on either side of the road. I can imagine myself wearing my thin white windcheater, standing on the road. I never move, never walk. I just watch the road and the lamp and the blinking lights.

I can't see it, but I can _feel_ that my house is nearby. Maybe on the other side of the curve. I imagine the Army quarters painted in white and my cozy warm room nested in there. I can imagine those yellow lamps old Army houses had outside, ones with a metal grid in front of the glass for a reason that I never figured out.

This place has remained permanent for a few years inside my constantly moving brain. But I'm yet to know if this is me being nostalgic towards something I have experienced before, or a yearning for somewhere I wish to be. I can't tell if it's a glimpse from my past or my future. All I can feel for it is _yearning_. I miss it in a way.

I sometimes wonder -- out of fear -- if I'm actually seeing the place where I will die. I _am_ standing in the middle of the road after all. I have never thought about vehicles in this place, but maybe that's the oversight that costs me my life. But it's too morose to taint that nostalgic place with fear, so I just continue to leave it to its mystery.

This place I'm unsure of continues to stay in my heart. And I go stand in that place from time to time; maybe because standing alone there feels better than standing alone here.
