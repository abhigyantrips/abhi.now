We sit atop a hill in Kilimanjaro. The trek here was long, and my legs are not going to move now that we have stopped to rest. We will probably set up a tent for the night here.

Vishnu is gazing at the sunset, while I'm gazing at him. The orange glow reflects from his pupils as he continues to stare at the dimming light.

"I want to enjoy every sunset from here on out," he says, "There's only so many of them left anyway."

I frown. "Do you mean because the world feels like it's ending?" I ask. A lot of our journey these few years had been off-grid, and our connection to the rest of the world was limited to the few times a rest stop had a TV with the news playing on it.

The glimpses from the news weren't cheerful. There was a lot of talk about data centers and forest fires, about brutality and oppression. It was strange to be detached from it all, from something that seemingly affected everyone in the world but us.

Vishnu continued to stare at the Sun, which had begun to conceal itself behind one of the hills. The silhouette of the mountain created a shadow tent around us.

"Whether the world ends or I end before it, the sunsets are limited anyway," he finally said.

The response froze me for a long time. We sat in the growing dark a little longer, as I slowly understood that the orange glow in his eyes could very well be from watching a burning fire one day.

Vishnu got up and dusted himself off. "Let's set up camp, Shiva," he said. "It's going to get cold soon."
