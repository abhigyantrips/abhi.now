I was recently taking a metro in Bengaluru to meet some friends. On entering the station and passing through the metal scanner, the security guard asked me to remove my mask. When I asked why, he said it was "for the camera".

_...Wait what?_

Mass surveillance and facial recognition have been a growing concern with their establishment in Western countries like the United States, but their presence in India is still new and crude. This notion was reinforced with the unreliability of the facial scans conducted during the Cockroach Janta Party protest, which were conducted out of a vendor-built surveillance van without a recorded policy[^1].

_But why is mass-surveillance growing all of a sudden?_

I mentioned the metro incident in IndieWebClub, which brought about a thread of discussion. This was one of the points made by Ashwin:

> [Governments] will always want more surveillance. It is upto the society to resist. That tension therein is a good thing and we arrive at a balance that does okay for both national security and for individual privacy.
>
> For some reason, the Indian society does not resist. We are happy to give [our Aadhaar card] everywhere, [phone number] everywhere, comply with [the government's] irrational requirements, etc.

I started out replying with some thoughts I had, which soon turned into _many_ thoughts. I realized I need to give them the time and space to exist, which brings me to writing this blogpost.

The question of the day, is _"Why does it seem like the general public in India doesn't care what the government is doing?"_. My short answer is that _they genuinely don't have the time (or patience) to do so_.

#### Text

"I think privacy/disagreement with the government is currently gatekept through privilege of education and economy.

The housekeeper/security guard/gig worker working 12-16 hours a day doesn't have the time or space to think about larger implications of laws, and would provide any information required if that reduces the friction they have to face that day.

India becoming the labour hub for the world is only increasing the desire to remove friction. I think the quick commerce/subscription service/but-now-pay-later models understand that deeply and are trying to exploit it.

Even the 'social media is bad' train of thought requires the time and space to observe your reactions to being online. I've noticed workers with long hours and high pressure tend to doomscroll a lot. That probably depends on wanting an escape from that pressure too, and increases impatience as we've seen through studies before."
