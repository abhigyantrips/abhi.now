Let me share with you an example of an ad my grandparents received, while searching for and playing a normal video on YouTube.

<div class="max-w-3/4 mt-6 mx-auto text-(--color-text-muted) italic">
  Your PDF app is not up-to-date! This can cause harm to your device and slow it down. To fix this issue, click on the link below to download the latest update and read PDF files smoothly.
</div>

This was delivered right before the video on a rainbow Instagram "Create Mode" background, with a link to an app on the Google Play Store.

All caught up? Alright, take a deep breath...

**_Why the hell are scams being shown directly to senior citizens?_**

This, by far, is not the first _or_ the worst example that has probably shown up for them. But nothing of this sort has ever popped up on _my_ feed as a young adult, and the only reason I can think of is that these ads are being targeted based on age.

### Young Adults & Companionship

It started with dating apps, where a plethora of Silicon Valley ventures tried to find you your soulmate. The effort turned into an unfulfilled promise, until eventually people learned (very publicly) about how finding you "the one" is not financially profitable for them. People started posting diagrams about success rates on Tinder, and even Hinge's "designed to be deleted" policy turned out to be hollow.
