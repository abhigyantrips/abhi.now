### The _Lathis_

The barricades were specially made for large crowds – over six feet tall, so people couldn't jump them or topple them over. The people would end up proving them wrong, and in the process learn that the barricades had live electricity running through them to shock the protesters. These walls of yellow and red were placed every hundred meters or so between Jantar Mantar and the Parliament.

The preparation didn't end at moral means, ofcourse. There were multiple reported sightings early morning of broken vans being planted around the protest site – untouched by protesters themselves – which would later on be labeled as "vandalism by the peaceful protest". There would be much brutality; the police would soon realize that their bamboo _lathis_ were not enough, and switch to ones made of fiberglass that are more durable and hurt more.

### The Journalists

Before the Sansad, the protest had continued with pin-drop silence from mainstream media. The topic was to be left alone, in a way that makes viewers question whether the movement is large enough -- or worse -- if it's even real.

Independent media did not sit still, though. The movement received extensive coverage from creators with tiny wireless microphones in their hand, asking people about the motives and how it affects them.
